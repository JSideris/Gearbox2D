\title{Kinematic Restitution Balancing (KRB)\\\large A Per-Constraint Energy Audit for Velocity-Level Impulse Solvers}

\author{Joshua Sideris\\Gear3Games\\\href{https://gearbox2d.com}{https://gearbox2d.com}\\\href{mailto:josh.sideris@gmail.com}{josh.sideris@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\small
Velocity-level sequential-impulse solvers gain energy from two analytic leaks: restitution and joint bias see the post-force velocity, and Baumgarte displacement does potential-energy work without a matching kinetic-energy tax. Kinematic Restitution Balancing (KRB) is a per-constraint \texttt{preSolve} audit of those leaks: Component~A subtracts the force increment from the impact velocity, and Component~B adjusts launch or bias speed for the expected correction $\Delta h$. A compile-time on/off ablation in Gearbox2D keeps a single elastic floor bounce at $E/E_0 \approx 1$ over $600\,\mathrm{s}$ (KRB off finishes at $6.53$) and holds a high-rate enclosure in-box; a Newton's cradle remains a bounded offset, not an invariant. The method adds no solver passes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Velocity-level sequential-impulse (SI) solvers \cite{Catto2005} are the standard real-time rigid-body approach: constraints are solved after explicit force integration with a fixed iteration count. KRB is a drop-in \emph{preSolve} energy audit for that pipeline. It does not add solver passes, replace PGS, or switch to position-based dynamics.

Bender, Erleben, and Trinkle survey interactive rigid-body practice \cite{Bender2014}. Baumgarte stabilization \cite{Baumgarte1972} and split impulse / NGS position passes \cite{Catto2014,Catto2024} hide bounce-from-correction but do not tax PE work of the remaining $\Delta h$; TGS, soft constraints, and XPBD \cite{Catto2011,PhysX2024,Macklin2016,Muller2007} damp or sidestep velocity-level bias at different cost. Variational integrators \cite{Hairer2006} bound unconstrained drift; KRB is not one of those. Among the SI variants cited here, none apply an analytic PE/KE balance to expected $\Delta h$ for both contacts and distance joints inside a single-pass impulse solver.

Two analytic sources inject artificial mechanical energy during constraint resolution.

\paragraph{Force-integration drift.}
With symplectic Euler integration, velocity is updated before the constraint solve:
\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{solver}} = v_t + a_{\mathrm{ext}} \cdot \Delta t.
\label{eq:force_drift}
\end{equation}
Restitution and joint bias see $v_{\mathrm{solver}}$ instead of the true relative velocity $v_{\mathrm{impact}}$. For a perfect bounce ($e=1$), the launch velocity gains $a_{\mathrm{ext}} \cdot \Delta t$ extra speed per frame. For a joint at rest, the solver sees gravity-induced velocity and fights it every frame at the velocity level.

\paragraph{Potential-energy shift from position correction.}
Baumgarte stabilization \cite{Baumgarte1972} feeds constraint error back as a velocity bias $v_{\mathrm{bias}} = \beta C / \Delta t$, displacing bodies by $\Delta h$ to resolve penetration or joint drift. That displacement increases potential energy ($mgh$) without reducing kinetic energy. The added PE converts to KE on later frames, causing runaway energy growth.

KRB applies that PE/KE balance to expected $\Delta h$ at constraint setup for contacts and distance joints. The audit is per-constraint; coupled graphs can still offset (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

KRB performs two independent corrections during constraint \texttt{preSolve}.

\subsection{Component A: Force velocity compensation}
\label{sec:component_a}

With symplectic Euler, integration updates velocity before the constraint solve (Equation~\ref{eq:force_drift}), so restitution and joint bias see $v_{\mathrm{solver}}$ rather than the pre-force relative velocity. Store the per-body force increment $v_{\mathrm{force}} = a_{\mathrm{ext}} \cdot \Delta t$ during integration and subtract it from the relative velocity seen at setup. That recovers the true impact normal velocity before restitution or bias is applied:
\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{impact}} = v_{\mathrm{relative}} - (v_{\mathrm{force},B} - v_{\mathrm{force},A}).
\label{eq:component_a}
\end{equation}
The correction is independent of the bias method.

\subsection{Component B: Kinematic energy balancing}
\label{sec:component_b}

After Component~A, tax (or credit) the launch speed for the potential-energy work of this step's expected correction $\Delta h$ (Section~\ref{sec:deff}). For a dynamic body on a static floor, center-of-mass motion along $\mathbf{n}$, free-flight rewind is $v_{\mathrm{impact}}^2 = v_{\mathrm{surf}}^2 - 2(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{n})\Delta h$. Equivalently, $\Delta E_p = -m(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{n})\Delta h$ taken from $\tfrac12 m v^2$ gives $\Delta(v^2) = 2(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{n})\Delta h$, so $m$ cancels. The implementation uses the relative normal acceleration $\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{rel}} = \mathbf{a}_B - \mathbf{a}_A$ (Listing~\ref{lst:contact}, $\texttt{forceVn}/\Delta t$):
\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{surf}} = \sqrt{\max\bigl(0,\, v_{\mathrm{impact}}^2 + 2 (\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{rel}} \cdot \mathbf{n}) \Delta h\bigr)},
\qquad v_{\mathrm{final}} = e\, v_{\mathrm{surf}}.
\label{eq:vsurf}
\end{equation}
Ground collisions ($\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{rel}}\cdot\mathbf{n}<0$) tax launch speed; ceiling collisions credit it. If both bodies share the same $\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{ext}}$, $\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{rel}}=\mathbf{0}$ and Component~B is a no-op. Rotation, tangent motion, and impulse work are outside this identity (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Effective displacement prediction}
\label{sec:deff}

Contacts and distance joints share Equation~\eqref{eq:vsurf}; they do not share $\Delta h$.

For a bouncing contact, the kinematic bounce $v_{\mathrm{launch}} = -e\cdot v_{\mathrm{impact}}$ clears some overlap before the position pass. After slop $s$,
\begin{align}
d_{\mathrm{eff}} &= \max\bigl(0,\, (d - s) - v_{\mathrm{launch}}\Delta t\bigr),
\label{eq:deff}\\
\Delta h &= \min(d_{\mathrm{eff}}, \Delta h_{\max})\cdot\Gamma,
\label{eq:deltah}
\end{align}
with $\Gamma = 1-(1-\beta)^n$ over $n$ position iterations. Distance joints use the bias travel $\Delta h_{\mathrm{joint}} = \beta C$ (Section~\ref{sec:joints}), not $\Gamma$.

These $\Delta h$ values are explicit predictors of Baumgarte travel, not a measurement of the later position pass. Using the pre-tax $v_{\mathrm{launch}}$ keeps $\Delta h$ closed-form; a post-tax $v_{\mathrm{final}}$ would make it implicit. On a ground tax the predictor is then slightly low---same sign as the original leak. $\Gamma$ is the isolated geometric series, not a fit to coupled NGS. If $v_{\mathrm{launch}}\Delta t$ already clears $(d-s)$, $d_{\mathrm{eff}}=0$ and there is no loop.

\section{Constraint application}
\label{sec:constraints}

\subsection{Contacts and speculative gaps}
\label{sec:contacts}

For physical overlap ($d > 0$), both Component~A and Component~B are required.

Speculative contacts are created before overlap ($d < 0$, a gap) to prevent tunneling. Because no position-correction work occurs yet, $\Delta h = 0$ and Component~B is omitted; Component~A remains critical so gravity-induced velocity is not treated as impact speed.

Apply restitution bias only when restitution is enabled and either the corrected normal velocity exceeds a small threshold, or a speculative gap is closing faster than the gap width allows. Resting contacts must not receive an $e=1$ launch bias.

\subsection{Distance joints and the zero-velocity paradox}
\label{sec:joints}

Distance joints target zero relative velocity along the rod. Omitting Component~B entirely leaks energy when Baumgarte stretch correction does work against gravity. Component~B must be applied, but capped so a resting joint is not paralyzed.

Let $v_{\mathrm{bias}} = \beta C / \Delta t$ be the ideal correction velocity for constraint error $C$. The audited correction is
\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{bias\_actual}} = \sqrt{\max\bigl(0,\, v_{\mathrm{bias}}^2 - 2 (\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{rel}} \cdot \mathbf{n}) (\beta C)\bigr)}.
\label{eq:joint_bias}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:joint_bias} is Equation~\eqref{eq:vsurf} with joint predictor $\Delta h_{\mathrm{joint}} = \beta C$: the travel implied by $v_{\mathrm{bias}} = \beta C / \Delta t$, not $\Gamma$ and not the position pass. Slop, the per-step $\Delta h_{\max}$ clamp, and extra joint position iterations when contacts overlap are uncaptured PE work (already inside the cradle residual). If kinetic energy cannot pay the PE tax, the joint retains a microscopic Baumgarte sag---bounded audit rather than infinite stiffness at rest. This paper's setup recipe covers contacts and \emph{distance} joints; other joint types are outside the listed implementation.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}

KRB runs at contact and distance-joint \texttt{preSolve}, before the velocity iteration loop. Prerequisites: symplectic Euler with per-body \texttt{forceVelocity} ($a_{\mathrm{ext}} \Delta t$), Baumgarte position correction with factor $\beta$, position iteration count $n$, and penetration slop.

\paragraph{Integrate store.}
Per dynamic body at integrate (before the constraint solve), store $v_{\mathrm{force}}$ (\texttt{forceVelocity}):
$v_{\mathrm{force}} = a_{\mathrm{ext}}\Delta t$ (two floats).
Here $a_{\mathrm{ext}}$ is gravity plus external force before linear damping; the store is zero for sleeping or infinite-mass bodies.

\subsection{Contact setup}
\label{sec:impl_contacts}

	basicstyle=\footnotesize\ttfamily,
	aboveskip=2pt,
	belowskip=2pt,

	breaklines=true,

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Contact \texttt{preSolve} bias (Component A + B).},label={lst:contact}]
// restitution threshold = 0.01; maxPosCorr = 0.2 (dH max)
forceVn = dot(bodyB.forceVelocity - bodyA.forceVelocity, n)
relativeVn = vn - forceVn
vBounce = -e * relativeVn
shouldBounce = enableRestitution &&
  (relativeVn < -RESTITUTION_THRESHOLD ||
   (depth < 0 && relativeVn < depth / dt))
if (shouldBounce) {
  if (depth > 0) {
    depthAfterVelocity = max(0, (depth - slop) - vBounce * dt)
    cumulativeFactor = 1 - pow(1 - beta, n)
    expectedDisplacement =
      min(depthAfterVelocity, maxPosCorr) * cumulativeFactor
  } else { expectedDisplacement = 0 }
  workTerm = 2 * (forceVn / dt) * expectedDisplacement
  vFinal = e * sqrt(max(0, relativeVn * relativeVn + workTerm))
  bias = (depth < 0) ? -max(vFinal, depth / dt) : -vFinal
} else if (depth < 0) { bias = -depth / dt } else { bias = 0 }
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Distance-joint setup}
\label{sec:impl_joints}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Distance-joint \texttt{preSolve} bias (Component A + B).},label={lst:joint}]
forceVn = dot(bodyB.forceVelocity - bodyA.forceVelocity, n)
v_bias_ideal = beta * C / dt
expectedDisplacement = v_bias_ideal * dt
workTerm = 2 * (forceVn / dt) * expectedDisplacement
v_bias_sq = v_bias_ideal * v_bias_ideal
v_bias_actual = sqrt(max(0, v_bias_sq - workTerm))
bias = (v_bias_ideal > 0 ? v_bias_actual : -v_bias_actual) - forceVn
\end{lstlisting}

During the velocity solve, $-\mathrm{forceVn}$ is folded into \texttt{bias} so \texttt{relative\_vn} is not double-corrected (see the supplement distance-joint listing).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

KRB is a per-constraint audit, not a coupled one: resolving one constraint can force work on another, as when a horizontal collision lifts a pendulum rod. In the Newton's cradle that shows up as a step from $\sim 0.96$ to $\sim 1.05$ near four minutes (Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}c); the $8\,\mathrm{h}$ band is in Table~\ref{tab:protocol} and Figure~\ref{fig:dataset-c}a. The Dataset~C enclosure stays in-box; $600\,\mathrm{s}$ windowed $E/E_0$ drifts $1.006 \to 0.976$ (Figure~\ref{fig:dataset-c}b), a slow loss of about $0.030\,E_0$ rather than the KRB-off SI gain of Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}b.

Energy results use the protocol in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}: single-pass SI/PGS, symplectic Euler, contacts and distance joints, $e = 1$, zero friction and damping, sleep off, and the stock World defaults and \texttt{constants.h} knobs. That meter is the setting where the two leaks in Section~\ref{sec:introduction} are visible. Friction dissipates, and $e<1$ skips the bounce tax whenever \texttt{shouldBounce} is false, so either would mask the audit rather than test it. The step $dt=1/60$ with 8 velocity and 3 position iterations is the usual real-time SI deploy (Box2D's suggested counts); $\Gamma$ uses that deployed $n$. Other $dt$ and iteration counts were not swept.

A coupled-constraint audit, tunneling, and long ill-conditioned chains remain future work, as do other integrators, hinge joints, and a switch to XPBD or TGS. Variational integrators \cite{Hairer2006} are a different claim.

\section{Conclusion}

KRB is a \texttt{preSolve} audit for sequential-impulse solvers \cite{Catto2005}: Component~A subtracts symplectic-Euler force drift from the impact velocity, and Component~B taxes the expected correction $\Delta h$. Contact scenes in the Gearbox2D ablation stay bounded; the coupled-constraint gap is in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
