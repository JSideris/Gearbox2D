\author{Joshua Sideris\\Gear3Games\\\href{https://gearbox2d.com}{https://gearbox2d.com}\\\href{mailto:josh.sideris@gmail.com}{josh.sideris@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\small
In discrete physics simulations using velocity-level impulse solvers (Sequential Impulse / Projected Gauss-Seidel), energy gain is a common numerical artifact. This paper introduces \textbf{Kinematic Restitution Balancing (KRB)}, a per-constraint correction for that leak in both unilateral constraints (collisions) and bilateral constraints (joints). By compensating for force-induced velocity drift (Component~A) and auditing potential-energy shifts during position correction (Component~B), KRB removes the two usual analytic energy sources inside a single-pass SI/PGS pipeline, without a second position pass. It does not claim a global energy invariant; evaluation and limitations sections state the coupled-constraint gap that follows from the per-constraint audit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Velocity-level sequential-impulse (SI) solvers \cite{Catto2005} are the standard real-time rigid-body approach: constraints are solved after explicit force integration with a fixed iteration count. KRB is a drop-in \emph{preSolve} energy audit for that pipeline. It does not add solver passes, replace PGS, or switch to position-based dynamics.

Interactive rigid-body practice is surveyed by Bender, Erleben, and Trinkle \cite{Bender2014}. Against that map: Baumgarte stabilization \cite{Baumgarte1972} and split impulse / nonlinear Gauss--Seidel (NGS) position passes \cite{Catto2014,Catto2024} hide immediate bounce-from-correction but do not tax the potential-energy work of the remaining displacement $\Delta h$; soft constraints, temporal Gauss--Seidel (TGS), and XPBD are alternative dampers \cite{Catto2011,PhysX2024,Macklin2016}. Dual-pass and TGS schemes \cite{PhysX2024} trade extra cost per step for better convergence. Position Based Dynamics and XPBD \cite{Muller2007,Macklin2016} sidestep velocity-level restitution bias but are a different solver family. Geometric and variational integrators \cite{Hairer2006} bound unconstrained Hamiltonian drift; KRB is not one of those. To our knowledge, prior velocity-level practice either accepts Baumgarte gain, hides bounce with a second position pass, or leaves the SI stack for PBD/XPBD---none apply an analytic PE/KE balance to the expected correction displacement $\Delta h$ at setup for both contacts and joints inside a single-pass impulse solver.

Two analytic sources inject artificial mechanical energy during constraint resolution.

\paragraph{Force-integration drift.}
With symplectic Euler integration, velocity is updated before the constraint solve:
\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{solver}} = v_t + a_{\mathrm{ext}} \cdot \Delta t.
\label{eq:force_drift}
\end{equation}
Restitution and joint bias see $v_{\mathrm{solver}}$ instead of the true relative velocity $v_{\mathrm{impact}}$. For a perfect bounce ($e=1$), the launch velocity gains $a_{\mathrm{ext}} \cdot \Delta t$ extra speed per frame. For a joint at rest, the solver sees gravity-induced velocity and fights it every frame at the velocity level.

\paragraph{Potential-energy shift from position correction.}
Baumgarte stabilization \cite{Baumgarte1972} feeds constraint error back as a velocity bias $v_{\mathrm{bias}} = \beta C / \Delta t$, displacing bodies by $\Delta h$ to resolve penetration or joint drift. That displacement increases potential energy ($mgh$) without reducing kinetic energy. The added PE converts to KE on later frames, causing runaway energy growth.

Component~B closes the leftover $\Delta h$ audit gap inside a single-pass SI profile (see placement map above).

KRB applies an analytic PE/KE balance to the \emph{expected} correction displacement $\Delta h$ at constraint setup, for contacts and distance joints. The audit is per-constraint; coupled graphs can still exhibit offsets when one constraint forces work on another (see Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

KRB performs two independent corrections during constraint \texttt{preSolve}.

\subsection{Component A: Force velocity compensation}
\label{sec:component_a}

With symplectic Euler, integration updates velocity before the constraint solve (Equation~\ref{eq:force_drift}), so restitution and joint bias see $v_{\mathrm{solver}}$ rather than the pre-force relative velocity. Store the per-body force increment $v_{\mathrm{force}} = a_{\mathrm{ext}} \cdot \Delta t$ during integration and subtract it from the relative velocity seen at setup. That recovers the true impact normal velocity before restitution or bias is applied:
\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{impact}} = v_{\mathrm{relative}} - (v_{\mathrm{force},B} - v_{\mathrm{force},A}).
\label{eq:component_a}
\end{equation}
Component~A applies to all constraints whenever symplectic Euler integration is used, independent of the bias method. In the implementation listing (Listing~\ref{lst:contact}), \texttt{relativeVn} is this corrected normal velocity after subtracting \texttt{forceVn}.

\subsection{Component B: Kinematic energy balancing}
\label{sec:component_b}

Baumgarte position correction displaces bodies by an expected distance $\Delta h$ along the constraint normal. External forces do work $W = m (\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{ext}} \cdot \mathbf{n}) \Delta h$ over that displacement. To keep mechanical energy consistent, the launch or bias speed must reflect that work: using $\Delta(\tfrac12 m v^2) = W$ gives a surface speed
$v_{\mathrm{surf}}^2 = v_{\mathrm{impact}}^2 + 2 (\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{ext}} \cdot \mathbf{n}) \Delta h$.
When available kinetic energy cannot pay the tax, clamp with $\max(0,\cdot)$ and apply restitution:
\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{surf}} = \sqrt{\max\bigl(0,\, v_{\mathrm{impact}}^2 + 2 (\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{ext}} \cdot \mathbf{n}) \Delta h\bigr)},
\label{eq:vsurf}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{final}} = e \cdot v_{\mathrm{surf}} = e \sqrt{\max\bigl(0,\, v_{\mathrm{impact}}^2 + 2 (\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{ext}} \cdot \mathbf{n}) \Delta h\bigr)}.
\label{eq:vfinal}
\end{equation}
For ground collisions ($\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{ext}} \cdot \mathbf{n} < 0$), Component~B taxes launch velocity to pay for increased PE; for ceiling collisions ($\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{ext}} \cdot \mathbf{n} > 0$), it credits velocity for work against external forces.

\subsection{Effective displacement prediction}
\label{sec:deff}

$\Delta h$ is the expected normal displacement used in the Component~B work term---not the full penetration depth, but the portion the position solver will actually correct this step after accounting for launch motion and per-iteration caps. When velocity and position phases are decoupled, the kinematic bounce velocity $v_{\mathrm{launch}}$ partially resolves penetration $d$ before the position solver runs. The remaining overlap after that motion is the effective depth
\begin{equation}
d_{\mathrm{eff}} = \max\bigl(0,\, d - (v_{\mathrm{launch}} \cdot \Delta t)\bigr).
\label{eq:deff}
\end{equation}
Many engines clamp per-iteration position correction to $\Delta h_{\max}$.
For example, Gearbox2D uses \texttt{MAX\_POSITION\_CORRECTION}.
The audit must match the work actually performed:
\begin{equation}
\Delta h = \min(d_{\mathrm{eff}}, \Delta h_{\max}) \cdot \Gamma,
\label{eq:deltah}
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma = 1 - (1 - \beta)^n$ is the cumulative Baumgarte fraction applied over $n$ position iterations with factor $\beta$.

\section{Constraint application}
\label{sec:constraints}

\subsection{Contacts and speculative gaps}
\label{sec:contacts}

For physical overlap ($d > 0$), both Component~A and Component~B are required.

Speculative contacts are created before overlap ($d < 0$, a gap) to prevent tunneling. Because no position-correction work occurs yet, $\Delta h = 0$ and Component~B is omitted; Component~A remains critical so gravity-induced velocity is not treated as impact speed.

Apply restitution bias only when restitution is enabled and either the corrected normal velocity exceeds a small threshold, or a speculative gap is closing faster than the gap width allows. Resting contacts must not receive an $e=1$ launch bias.

\subsection{Distance joints and the zero-velocity paradox}
\label{sec:joints}

Distance joints target zero relative velocity along the rod. Omitting Component~B entirely leaks energy when Baumgarte stretch correction does work against gravity. Component~B must be applied, but capped so a resting joint is not paralyzed.

Let $v_{\mathrm{bias}} = \beta C / \Delta t$ be the ideal correction velocity for constraint error $C$. The audited correction is
\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{bias\_actual}} = \sqrt{\max\bigl(0,\, v_{\mathrm{bias}}^2 - 2 (\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{ext}} \cdot \mathbf{n}) (\beta C)\bigr)}.
\label{eq:joint_bias}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:joint_bias} is the same PE/KE audit with expected stretch displacement $\beta C$ in place of contact $\Delta h$. If kinetic energy cannot pay the PE tax, the joint retains a microscopic Baumgarte sag---bounded audit rather than infinite stiffness at rest. This paper's setup recipe covers contacts and \emph{distance} joints; other joint types are outside the listed implementation.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}

KRB runs at contact and distance-joint \texttt{preSolve}, before the velocity iteration loop. Prerequisites: symplectic Euler with per-body \texttt{forceVelocity} ($a_{\mathrm{ext}} \Delta t$), Baumgarte position correction with factor $\beta$, position iteration count $n$, and penetration slop.

\subsection{Contact setup}
\label{sec:impl_contacts}

\lstset{basicstyle=\footnotesize\ttfamily,aboveskip=3pt,belowskip=3pt}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Contact \texttt{preSolve} bias (Component A + B).},label={lst:contact}]
// Component A: true impact normal velocity
forceVn = dot(bodyB.forceVelocity - bodyA.forceVelocity, n)
relativeVn = vn - forceVn

vBounce = -e * relativeVn
shouldBounce = enableRestitution &&
  (relativeVn < -RESTITUTION_THRESHOLD ||
   (depth < 0 && relativeVn < depth / dt))

if (shouldBounce) {
  if (depth > 0) {
    depthAfterVelocity = max(0, (depth - slop) - vBounce * dt)
    cumulativeFactor = 1 - pow(1 - beta, n)
    expectedDisplacement =
      min(depthAfterVelocity, MAX_POSITION_CORRECTION) *
      cumulativeFactor
  } else {
    expectedDisplacement = 0  // speculative: Component A only
  }
  accVn = forceVn / dt
  workTerm = 2 * accVn * expectedDisplacement
  vSurfSq = relativeVn * relativeVn + workTerm
  vFinal = e * sqrt(max(0, vSurfSq))
  bias = (depth < 0) ? -max(vFinal, depth / dt) : -vFinal
} else if (depth < 0) {
  bias = -depth / dt
} else {
  bias = 0
}
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Distance-joint setup}
\label{sec:impl_joints}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Distance-joint \texttt{preSolve} bias.},label={lst:joint},language=C++]
float forceVn =
  (bodyB->forceVelocity - bodyA->forceVelocity).dot(normal);
float v_bias_ideal = beta * C / dt;
float expectedDisplacement = v_bias_ideal * dt;
float accVn = forceVn / dt;
float workTerm = 2.0f * accVn * expectedDisplacement;
float v_bias_sq = v_bias_ideal * v_bias_ideal;
float adjusted_v_bias_sq =
  max(0.0f, v_bias_sq - workTerm);
float v_bias_actual = sqrt(adjusted_v_bias_sq);
bias = (v_bias_ideal > 0 ? v_bias_actual : -v_bias_actual) - forceVn;
\end{lstlisting}

During the velocity solve, use the stored bias; Component~A's $-\mathrm{forceVn}$ is folded into \texttt{bias} so \texttt{relative\_vn} is not double-corrected each iteration.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

Four datasets: Datasets~A--B energy runs last $600\,\mathrm{s}$; Dataset~C KRB-on continuation lasts $8\,\mathrm{h}$ ($t = 28800\,\mathrm{s}$); Dataset~D reports native \texttt{world.step()} wall-time only (Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}: compile-time KRB on/off; Figure~\ref{fig:engines}: whole-engine traces; traces in \texttt{data/dataset-c/} and \texttt{data/timing/}). Not a patched-Box2D result.

Energy claims C01--C07 use $dt = 1/60$, $e = 1$, zero friction and damping, sleep disabled throughout. Components~A and~B are an analytic two-leak decomposition; the only empirical switch is compile-time full KRB versus \texttt{-DGEARBOX\_DISABLE\_KRB} (no isolated A-only or B-only traces). Mechanical energy is $E = E_k + E_p$; Dataset~A includes rotational KE, Dataset~B is translational only (the website adapters do not expose $\omega$); on the overlapping Gearbox floor-bounce traces the two agree to about $10^{-6}$. \subsection{Cost (Dataset D)}
\label{sec:dataset-d}

The ``Low-Cost'' claim is checkable without a second solver pass. Per dynamic body at integrate, KRB stores $\mathrm{forceVelocity} = a_{\mathrm{ext}} \cdot \Delta t$ (two floats). Per contact \texttt{preSolve} when KRB is on: a $\mathrm{forceVn}$ dot, optional $\Gamma$, a \texttt{workTerm}, one \texttt{sqrt}, and a bias update. Per distance-joint \texttt{preSolve}: $\mathrm{forceVn}$, \texttt{workTerm}, one \texttt{sqrt}, with $-\mathrm{forceVn}$ folded into \texttt{bias}. There are zero extra PGS, velocity, or position iterations---not a second position pass or coupled-island PE tax. \section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

KRB is a per-constraint audit, not a coupled one. When resolving one constraint (for example a horizontal collision) forces a second constraint to do work against gravity (for example a pendulum rod lifting the body), the isolated PE/KE tax may miss the systemic change in potential energy.

Energy evaluation is restricted to the $e = 1$, zero-friction, zero-damping, sleep-disabled protocol in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}; it is not a general frictional or $e < 1$ claim. No experimental Component~A-only or Component~B-only ablation was run; full KRB on/off is the measured switch.

Newton's cradle is that coupled case. In Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}c and Figure~\ref{fig:engines}b the KRB run shows a $\sim 7\%$ energy step near four minutes, then a plateau for the rest of the $600\,\mathrm{s}$ window---not a secular climb, and not a proof of a global invariant. Dataset~C continues that KRB-on cradle to $8\,\mathrm{h}$ of simulation time ($dt = 1/60$, sampled every $10\,\mathrm{s}$; traces in \texttt{data/dataset-c/}). After the same four-minute step, $E/E_0$ stays in $[1.034, 1.093]$ (mean $1.053$) through $t = 28800\,\mathrm{s}$; the last-hour mean is $1.053$, identical to the hour after the step, and $60\,\mathrm{s}$ windowed means do not jump again. That is an empirical multi-hour bound for this scene, still not a global invariant, and still not shown for arbitrary contact/joint graphs.

The same $600\,\mathrm{s}$ of unmodified Box2D, p2, and Matter (Figure~\ref{fig:engines}b) dissipate rather than gain; that is a different failure mode and does not close the coupled-audit gap. Variational integrators \cite{Hairer2006} remain a different claim.

A coupled-constraint audit is future work. High-speed tunneling, long ill-conditioned chains, and other scenes that fail for reasons other than the two leaks in Section~\ref{sec:introduction} are outside the claim. The no-KRB enclosure escape in Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}b is the SI leak at high impact rate, not a KRB failure.

\section{Conclusion}

Kinematic Restitution Balancing is a drop-in correction for velocity-level sequential-impulse solvers \cite{Catto2005}. Component~A removes force-integration drift from restitution and joint bias; Component~B taxes (or credits) the launch or bias velocity for the potential-energy work of the expected correction displacement $\Delta h$. Both apply to contacts and distance joints, at the cost of a few extra scalars per constraint setup (Table~\ref{tab:dataset-d}).

That is a narrower claim than a dual-pass solver or a reduced-coordinate formulation. Split impulse and NGS \cite{Catto2014,Catto2024} still hide Baumgarte bounce by keeping position work off the physical velocity; KRB does not replace that machinery. It cancels the two analytic leaks in Section~\ref{sec:introduction} inside a single-pass SI profile, with the coupled-constraint gap in Section~\ref{sec:limitations} left open.
